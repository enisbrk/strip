Scrolling window manager for macOS

Windows side by side, not piled up.

Strip turns every window into a column on a horizontal strip that runs past the edges of your screen. New windows open next to the focused one, the rest keep their size, and the strip slides one screen at a time.

Features

Everything stays in a line you can steer.

No overlapping, no hunting for windows behind other windows. Strip lays them out, you move along the strip.

An endless strip of columns

Every window is a column. New ones open right next to the one you're using, nothing gets resized behind your back, and the strip slides a whole screen at a time — a half-filled screen never shows the next one's windows.

Stacks & tabs

Stack windows into one column, resize them, or flip the stack into tabs when space is tight.

Edit strip⌃⌥O

The whole strip at a glance

Press ⌃⌥O and your windows step aside for a glass panel over the bare desktop, every screen a miniature display with its windows on it. Drag windows beside, above or below each other, drag whole screens into a new order, or pick one with the arrow keys and Return, like ⌘Tab.

Drop to share a screen

Drag a window onto another and they split its screen: two halves, then a half and two quarters, then four quarters. Close one and the windows left widen to fill the screen again — or turn that off to let the next screen slide in.

⅓½⅔1

Preset widths

Cycle through ⅓, ½, ⅔ and full width with one key, or nudge wider or narrower.

⌃⌥C

Centered, with margins

⌃⌥C gives a column a screen of its own, centered with empty margins, so the next window never peeks in.

Trackpad swipe

Glide along the strip with three or four fingers. When you let go it settles on the nearest screen.

⌃⌥L

Always know where you are

A minimap with each app's icon shows the whole strip and your place in it, whenever you press a shortcut or a window joins. Add a focus border, dim inactive windows, and see each shortcut as you press it.

Snap to edges & corners

Drag a window against a side or corner of the screen — no editor needed. A glass outline shows where it lands: a half, the top or bottom, or a quarter.

App rules & multiple displays

Keep Calculator or System Settings floating, give apps their own width and position, and send windows to the next display. Wi-Fi sign-in sheets stay on top, out of the strip.

$ strip focus-right
$ strip stack-right
$ open strip://run/edit-strip

Scriptable

Every command works from strip:// URLs, a strip command-line tool and ready-made Raycast script commands.

ENTRESFRIT

Private by design

Strip needs one permission — Accessibility — to move windows. It doesn't record or send anything. Available in five languages.

Keyboard first

Everything starts with ⌃ ⌥

Vim-style keys or arrows, your choice. Every shortcut can be changed in Settings.

  • HLFocus window left / right
  • ←→Slide to previous / next screen
  • ⇧HLMove window left / right
  • JKWindow below / above in stack
  • []Stack into column left / right
  • RCycle preset widths
  • FFull width
  • CCenter on its own screen
  • =−Wider / narrower
  • TFloat / tile window
  • 1…9Jump to window 1–9
  • NSend window to next display
  • OEdit strip: windows, screens, widths, stacks
  • QStop / start Strip
